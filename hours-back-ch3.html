Four gaps

Each line is one gap. The green dot is the group furthest ahead. The orange dot is the group furthest behind. Hover or tap a dot for the number.

Pick a gap and look closer

Bars in green mean most people in that group already use AI. Orange means most do not yet.

The desk is the doorway

This links straight back to chapter 2. ABS data shows 59% of managers and professionals usually work from home at least some of the time. For other jobs it is 21%. If your job happens on a laptop, AI is one tab away. If it happens on a site, in a truck or on a farm, it is not part of your day.

Small business shows the same pattern. About 43% of small and medium businesses use AI. In health, education and services it is above half. In construction and agriculture it is below 30%.

The surprise

People who speak a language other than English at home use AI more: 58.8%, against 41.4% for English only. When you work in your second language, a tool that checks an email or explains a form saves real time and real stress. I came here from Bangalore, so this one felt personal.

The gap is not about who is "smart enough". It is about who has a reason and a chance to try.

What is actually stopping people?

I assumed skill. The small business data says otherwise.

Who the help reaches

Public serviceEvery public servant gets AI training and access under the APS AI Plan.
UniversitiesAbout 80% of uni students used AI in 2025. ANU and Anthropic signed a A$500k partnership.
BusinessThe National AI Centre now tracks small business uptake sector by sector.

Good moves. But look who they reach: public servants, uni students, office workers. The groups already in green. The orange side of the chart is mostly not in these programs.

One more thing. Only 13.7% of Australians pay for a premium AI tool, and Australia skipped the last round of the OECD adult skills survey. The last data we have said about 44% of adults had low literacy. We are rolling out tools that reward strong reading, without fresh numbers on how many people read well.

My ask for this week

Show one person outside your bubble one task. A parent, a tradie mate, a neighbour with a small shop. Not "here is AI". Just "here is how I would write that quote, or read that letter". That is how trust gets built. One hour back at a time.
